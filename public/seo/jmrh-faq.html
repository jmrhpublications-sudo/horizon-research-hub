How to write a PhD synopsis?

A PhD synopsis typically includes: (1) Title of the research, (2) Introduction and background, (3) Review of literature highlighting the research gap, (4) Statement of the problem, (5) Research objectives and hypotheses, (6) Methodology in detail, (7) Expected outcomes and significance, (8) References. How to write a literature review for a research paper?

A strong literature review: (1) Identifies key themes and debates in your research area, (2) Critically evaluates existing studies rather than just summarizing, (3) Identifies gaps that justify your research, (4) Uses recent sources (preferably last 5-10 years), (5) Organizes content thematically, chronologically, or methodologically, (6) Synthesizes findings across multiple sources. Use Google Scholar, Scopus, and ResearchGate to locate relevant papers.

What is a DOI and how do I get one for my paper?

A DOI (Digital Object Identifier) is a unique alphanumeric string that provides a permanent link to your scholarly article online. DOIs are assigned by Crossref upon publication. They ensure your work remains findable even if URLs change. DOIs are now standard for all reputable academic publications and are required by most citation formats.

What is an ISSN and how is it different from ISBN?

ISSN (International Standard Serial Number) is an 8-digit code identifying serial publications like journals. ISBN (International Standard Book Number) is a 13-digit number for individual books. ISSN stays the same for the entire run of a journal; ISBN changes with each edition and format. ISSN is free in India; ISBN has a fee. Both are essential identifiers for academic publications.

What is an international journal and how to identify one?

An international journal accepts submissions from authors worldwide and features: (1) An international editorial board with members from multiple countries, (2) Global readership and distribution, (3) International indexing in Scopus, Web of Science, etc., (4) Standard publication practices including rigorous peer review, (5) ISSN registration, (6) DOI assignment via Crossref. International reach depends on global engagement, not just the publisher's location.

How does the peer review process work in academic journals?

Peer review process: (1) Author submits manuscript, (2) Editor performs initial screening for scope and quality, (3) Editor assigns to 2-3 expert reviewers, (4) Reviewers evaluate methodology, originality, significance, and clarity, (5) Reviewers submit recommendations (accept, minor revision, major revision, reject), (6) Editor makes final decision considering reviewer feedback, (7) Decision communicated to author with reviewer comments, (8) Author revises if required and resubmits for final approval.

How long does it take to publish a research paper?

Total range: 6-16 weeks. Some journals offer accelerated review options. Factors affecting timeline include reviewer availability, number of revision rounds, and journal workload.

What is plagiarism in academic publishing?

Plagiarism is using someone else's work, ideas, or words without proper attribution. Types include: (1) Direct plagiarism — copying text verbatim without quotation marks, (2) Mosaic plagiarism — changing some words while keeping the original structure, (3) Self-plagiarism — reusing your own published work without citation, (4) Accidental plagiarism — unintentional failure to cite sources. All forms violate academic integrity and can lead to manuscript rejection or retraction.

What is the acceptable plagiarism percentage for research papers?

General guidelines: Below 10% — excellent; 10-15% — acceptable for most journals; 15-25% — requires revision and explanation; Above 25% — likely rejected by most reputable journals. UGC recommends below 10% text similarity. However, the source of similarity matters — proper citations in references, standard phrases, and direct quotes are less concerning than copied body text without attribution.

What is the standard research paper format?

Standard format includes: (1) Title page with title, authors, and affiliations, (2) Abstract (150-250 words), (3) Keywords (4-6), (4) Introduction with literature review, (5) Research methodology, (6) Results and findings, (7) Discussion, (8) Conclusion, (9) References, (10) Appendices if needed. Always follow the target journal's specific formatting guidelines for font, spacing, citation style, and word count limits.

What is the difference between APA, MLA, and IEEE citation styles?

APA (American Psychological Association): Used in social sciences. Author-date in-text citations (Smith, 2020). Reference list ordered alphabetically. MLA (Modern Language Association): Used in humanities. Author-page in-text citations (Smith 45). Works Cited page. IEEE (Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers): Used in engineering and computer science. Numbered references in brackets [1]. Each style has specific rules for formatting different source types.
